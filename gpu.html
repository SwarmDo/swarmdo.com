What it is

A general-purpose GPU service. We’re just tenant zero.

SwarmDoGPU is not a SwarmDo accessory. It’s managed GPU compute for anyone’s workload — yours included, whatever it happens to be. We build it, and we also happen to be a demanding customer of it, which is the useful part.

Bring your own

Any workload

Your code, your framework, your model — or no model at all. Training, fine-tuning, inference, batch and offline jobs. Nothing about it is SwarmDo-specific.

Or ours

SwarmDo-A1 / A2

Our own open models are one workload among many — a first-party tenant, not the boundary of what the service runs.

Why that matters

Dogfooded

Running our own training and serving on it means the awkward parts get found by us first, rather than by you.

The gap it closes. Plenty of good work is gated on hardware nobody wants to buy. Our own models make the point: A1 and A2 are genuinely open — Apache-2.0 weights, no API lock-in — but “fits on one 80GB GPU” still assumes you have an 80GB GPU, and A2 has to be served merged under vLLM with specific parser and eager-mode flags. Every serious GPU workload has a cliff like that. Removing them is the job, regardless of whose code is running.

What it’s being built for

The workloads the ecosystem already generates.

The shapes of work it’s designed around. Our own jobs are in here, but only as one example — none of these assume you’re running anything of ours. Design targets, not a shipped feature list.

▲Training & fine-tuning

Jobs that need a big card for hours or days, and that you’d rather not buy hardware for. Your framework, your data, your checkpoints.

◉Inference & serving

Standing an endpoint up and keeping it up — whatever the model is, and whoever trained it. Open weights, your own, or something off the shelf.

⇉Batch & parallel work

Embarrassingly parallel jobs that want throughput more than one fast card: sweeps, evals, offline processing, render and verify loops.

⛁Anything else GPU-bound

It doesn’t have to be an LLM, or AI at all. If it needs a GPU and you don’t want to own one, that’s the use case. Tell us what yours is.

Our own workloads, as a worked example. SwarmDo-A1 and A2 are served from it, and their execution-verified patch selection and render-verified visual scoring are exactly the bursty, parallel, verify-heavy jobs described above. That’s why they shaped the design — not because the service is limited to them.

Honest status

What we don’t claim yet.

SwarmDo publishes null results and unfinished work rather than burying them. The same rule applies to this page.

  • No product is live. The SwarmDoGPU repository currently holds project scaffolding and integration config. Application code, usage instructions, and deployment docs are still to come.
  • No pricing is announced. There are no published tiers, rates, or limits — anything you read elsewhere claiming otherwise isn’t from us.
  • No benchmarks are claimed. We haven’t measured this service, so we won’t quote numbers for it. The numbers on this site belong to the models, and every one of them is reproducible.
  • No hardware is promised. Specific GPU types, regions, and availability will be stated when they’re real.
In the meantime our other two projects work today and are fully open: the plugin is npx swarmdo init, and the models are downloadable weights you can serve on your own hardware with no account at all.
